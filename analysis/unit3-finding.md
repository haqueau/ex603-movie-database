# Reviewer payouts: what the dashboard got wrong

**To:** The dashboard team
**Re:** The frozen reviewer payout

The dashboard reported that only 12 of our 30 reviewers had any ratings that count toward a payout, and it found just 22 qualifying ratings across the whole platform. That is wrong. In fact, all 30 reviewers have qualifying ratings, 188 in total, and only 12 ratings were removed as spam. Worse, the dashboard had it backwards: the 22 ratings it did count were all ratings that had already been taken down for other reasons, while every ordinary, published rating was left out. User_13 shows the effect most clearly. They are one of our most active reviewers, with 11 published ratings and no spam removals, yet the dashboard credited them with none. Their dispute is justified.

The error comes from how removals are recorded. Each rating has a "withdrawal reason" that is only filled in when the rating is taken down; for an ordinary rating, it is left blank. The dashboard's rule was to count every rating whose reason is "not spam." A person reads a blank reason as "not spam," but the database treats a blank as unknown. It cannot confirm that an unknown reason is not spam, so it quietly left every one of those ratings out. No error appeared and no warning was shown, so nobody noticed that 166 ratings were missing. We have corrected the rule to say plainly that a blank reason counts as not spam, and the corrected figures are the ones given above.

We recommend releasing the frozen payout in part. The 20 reviewers who have never had a rating removed as spam should be paid now, based on the corrected counts. The 10 reviewers with at least one spam removal should stay on hold until someone reviews their accounts, since spam is exactly what the payout rules are meant to guard against. One point needs a decision before final amounts are set: the corrected counts still include ratings removed for reasons other than spam, such as duplicates or policy violations, and the team should confirm whether those should earn anything. Going forward, any rule that checks the withdrawal reason should state explicitly what happens when the reason is blank.
