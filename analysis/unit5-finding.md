## Group 1: Movies that fail often (11, 9 and 2)

These three movies are rated as much as a typical title (14 to 16 ratings each),
but between a quarter and a third of their ratings are withdrawn, against 16.5%
for a normal movie.

I checked whether something about the movies themselves explains this, by
comparing each with the most similar titles in the catalog (same country of
origin or same release year). It does not. Movie 11's closest match, movie 12,
comes from the same country and year and shares a genre, yet loses only 12% of
its ratings against movie 11's 31%. Volume does not explain it either. That
points back to the causes found last unit, which are different for each movie:

- **Movie 2**: three ratings removed as spam, from three different accounts. This
  looks like a coordinated target.
- **Movie 11**: four of its five removals came from ratings made in India.
- **Movie 9**: removals spread across reasons and regions. It is also the only
  one of the three that is no longer available to watch.

**Recommended action: a targeted moderation review, led by Trust & Safety.**

1. Review the three spam accounts on movie 2 first, and check whether they have
   posted on other titles.
2. Check how ratings made from India are being moderated, starting with
   movie 11.
3. Before spending review time on movie 9, ask the catalog team why it was
   removed from the service. If it is not coming back, its ratings matter less.
4. Re-check the three failure rates in three months. Success means each is back
   near the platform normal of about 16.5%.

Promoting these movies would not help. They already get attention; the problem
is what happens to the ratings afterwards.

## Group 2: Movies that never started (14 and 15)

Movies 14 and 15 have been in the catalog since early 2022, are marked as
available, and have received **no ratings at all**, across a full year in which
every other available movie was rated at least 6 times.

What the data shows: neither movie has a genre. That means neither appears on
any genre page or in any genre-based recommendation, which makes them hard to
find. What the data does not show is whether that is the whole reason. One other
movie (13) also has no genre and still received 11 ratings. The platform also
records viewing only when someone leaves a rating, so these movies may have been
watched and simply never rated. Nothing in the data tells us whether they can
actually be streamed in every region where they are listed.

**Recommended action: make them findable, confirm they work, then decide.**

1. Have the catalog team assign genres to movies 14 and 15 (and to movie 13,
   which has the same gap).
2. Confirm with the content team that both movies can actually be played in
   every region where they are shown as available.
3. Re-check in 90 days. If they are still unrated once they are tagged and
   playable, review whether they are worth continuing to license.

Moderation would do nothing for these movies: there are no ratings to moderate.

## One change to how we report

Last unit's performance report started from the ratings, so it could only see
movies that had ratings, and movies 14 and 15 never appeared on it. Every
movie performance report should start from the full catalog, so that a title
with no activity shows up as zero instead of silently disappearing.

These figures come from a small dataset of 15 movies and 200 ratings, so a single
rating can move a movie's numbers noticeably. They show where to look first, not
a final verdict on any title.
